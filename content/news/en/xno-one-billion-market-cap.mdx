# What a $1B XNO Market Cap Would Look Like

Market cap math is simple, which is exactly why it is worth doing carefully. If every XNO were worth a certain price, what would the network look like? Let us take $1 billion as a round number and run the numbers.

## The arithmetic

Nano's supply is fixed at 133,248,297 XNO, and all of it is in circulation. There is no inflation to dilute this figure later, which makes the calculation stable. To reach a $1 billion market cap, the price of XNO would need to be about $7.50.

</Terminal>

That is the whole calculation. The interesting part is what it implies.

## What changes at that price

At $7.50, Nano would enter the top 100 cryptocurrencies by market cap, sitting alongside names most people have heard of.

<Callout type="insight" title="What changes">
	A transaction of 100 XNO becomes a $750 transaction. The mental math of what Nano is for shifts: the micropayment story moves toward everyday payments for goods and services.
</Callout>

None of this says anything about whether that price is likely. It just describes what the network would look like if it got there.

## What it would take

A market cap is the market's collective judgment, not a technical milestone. Reaching $1 billion would require sustained demand for actual use, not speculation alone. That means more wallets, more payment integrations, more places where Nano is accepted, and people who use it repeatedly because it works.

The good news is that the fundamentals required for that kind of adoption already exist: feeless transactions, instant settlement, and a fixed supply. What remains is distribution and utility, the slow work of getting Nano into more hands and more products.
