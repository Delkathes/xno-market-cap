# The environmental case for Nano

Crypto's electricity use gets audited regularly now, and almost all of the criticism lands on proof of work. Nano does not use it. That is most of the environmental argument in one sentence, but the numbers are worth spelling out.

## The problem with proof of work

Bitcoin and similar networks secure themselves with Proof-of-Work (PoW) mining, which:

- Consumes more electricity than some countries.
- Requires specialized hardware that ends up as e-waste.
- Turns electricity into a competition, since more hash rate is the only way to stay in the race.

## Nano's alternative: ORV

Nano uses Open Representative Voting (ORV) instead of mining:

- **No mining**: representatives vote on blocks instead of competing for them.
- **Minimal hardware**: a node runs on a standard server.
- **Fixed supply**: every Nano that will ever exist already exists, so there is nothing to mine.

## Energy comparison

| Network | Energy per transaction |
|---------|------------------------|
| Nano | 0.000112 kWh |
| Ethereum | 62.56 kWh |
| Bitcoin | 1,173 kWh |

By those figures, a Bitcoin transaction carries more than **10 million times** the energy of a Nano transaction.

## In everyday terms

<Callout type="insight" title="Real-world scale">A single Nano transaction uses less energy than sending an email, running a Google search, or charging your phone for one second.</Callout>

## Carbon footprint

The entire Nano network is estimated to run on the energy output of a single wind turbine, while still processing thousands of transactions per second. With no mining subsidy to chase, the electricity bill is the cost of running nodes.

## Sustainability

In practical terms, that means:

- A network whose security does not depend on burning more electricity.
- A smaller digital carbon footprint per transaction, by the numbers above.
- A counterexample to the assumption that crypto has to be an energy problem.
