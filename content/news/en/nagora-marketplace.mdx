# Nagora: a Buyer-Protected P2P Marketplace Built on Nano

A peer-to-peer marketplace where every trade is held in buyer-protected escrow is the kind of thing Nano should be good at. Nagora is the latest attempt to prove it. Developer Immi, who announced the project on r/nanocurrency, has built a marketplace where sellers list items, buyers pay in XNO, and the funds sit in escrow until the buyer confirms delivery. Payments settle in seconds with no network fees, and there are no chargebacks to reverse a trade after the fact.

The escrow is the differentiator, not the shop part. Anyone can bolt a storefront onto a crypto payment rail. Holding the funds in the middle of a trade between two strangers, with a dispute path when something goes wrong, is the part that usually gets skipped. Nagora puts it on every order, no exceptions.

## Sign-up without the KYC flow

Creating an account is an email and a password. No identity documents, no proof of address, no verification video calls. That keeps the barrier to entry low on both sides of the market: buyers and sellers can be live in minutes. The trade-off is the usual one, frictionless accounts attract more abuse, so the escrow and dispute process carry more weight than they would on a banked platform.

The first 15 sellers with an approved listing this month go a step further and earn Founder tier: 0% fees forever, plus a badge on their profile. It is a small, concrete reward for being early, and it gives the marketplace its first tier of identifiable sellers.

## Day-one stock

A marketplace with zero listings is a ghost town, so Nagora launched seeded. Amazon and Steam gift cards are already listed across USD, GBP, and EUR denominations, which means there is something real to buy on day one while the community fills in the rest. The marketplace is open: anyone can list anything, and the browse grid already spans electronics, collectibles, and services.

## Early days, honest assessment

This is a day-one marketplace from a solo developer, and it should be judged as one. The incentives are generous because they need to be. The escrow model means the operator holds funds during a trade, which is a trust relationship worth watching as volume grows. None of it is proven at scale yet, and Immi is openly treating the launch as a test: he asked for bug reports and harsh feedback, and promised to fix anything that breaks.

The full write-up with screenshots is on [r/nanocurrency](https://www.reddit.com/r/nanocurrency/comments/1sxz2nl/announcing_nagora_a_p2p_marketplace_for_nano/), and the marketplace itself is live at [nagora.shop](https://nagora.shop/). For a network that has spent years looking for its real-world use case, an escrowed marketplace is one of the more serious attempts yet. Worth watching how the first trades hold up.
