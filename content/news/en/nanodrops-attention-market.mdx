# Paid per minute to watch livestreams: the Nano attention market

There is now a market where watching a livestream pays you, and it runs on Nano. nanodrops.org calls itself "the earn-per-minute attention economy". Streamers fund a faucet for their stream, the site shows a live rate in XNO per hour, and viewers with a Nano address and a browser extension collect payouts for as long as they keep watching. Stouty, the developer behind the project, has been calling it the new meta in livestreaming, and the "Black Mirror" label is not hard to understand.

## Watching as paid work

The mechanics are deliberately blunt.

<Steps title="How the market works">
	<Step index="01" title="Fund">A streamer tops up a faucet with Nano. The payout rate ticks down as the faucet is drained by the people watching.</Step>
	<Step index="02" title="Watch">Viewers watch; the live rate ticks in XNO per hour.</Step>
	<Step index="03" title="Earn">Verified watch time pays out to a Nano address, with receipts on the site and a running "global paid" counter for everyone to see.</Step>
</Steps>

There is no sign-up wall and no ad network in the middle. The stream is the product, the attention is the payment, and the token that moves between them is XNO.

Creator platforms have spent two decades selling audience attention to advertisers. Here the direction of money is reversed: the streamer spends, and the viewer is paid for being the audience. Whether that reads as a dystopia or as a natural extension of what open money makes possible, it is a new shape for the attention economy.

## Why only Nano could run this

The per-minute detail is the whole point, and it only works on a network where micro-transactions are practical. Paying every viewer a fraction of a XNO every minute, thousands of times an hour, is a stream of tiny instant payments. On a network with fees, the infrastructure cost would eat the model alive. Nano is feeless, instant, and divisible down to absurd precision, which is exactly what a faucet that drips per minute needs.

The irony is that the site uses the two properties the crypto world usually brags about for speculation and turns them into labor. Here, feeless and instant are the product itself. An earn-per-minute market is only possible when the payment itself is cheap enough to be the smallest unit of attention.

## A small market with a strange question

nanodrops.org is not trying to replace Twitch or YouTube. It is an experiment in what happens when attention has a price that can be paid continuously. The numbers are small, the audience is early, and the "Black Mirror" framing is half the appeal.

The site itself lives at [nanodrops.org](https://nanodrops.org), with browser extensions for Chrome and Firefox and a leaderboard for the dedicated. It costs nothing to watch, and now watching can cost the streamer.
